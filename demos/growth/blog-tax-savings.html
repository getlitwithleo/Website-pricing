Setting money aside for taxes, without the stress

The most stressful tax bill is the one you didn't plan for. The easiest way to avoid it is to set money aside every time you get paid.

Rachel Kim · Founder, ClearpathOct 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Every spring we hear from business owners who had a good year and a terrible April. The money came in, it went out, and when the tax bill arrived there wasn't enough left to pay it. It's one of the most common, and most avoidable, problems in small business.

Step 1: open a separate account

Open a savings account that's only for taxes. Not your business checking, not your personal savings. A separate account you don't look at, with a name like "TAXES: DO NOT TOUCH."

Step 2: move a fixed share of every payment

Each time money comes in, move a set percentage into the tax account. Ask your accountant what share makes sense for your business. Many owners start somewhere between 20 and 30 percent and adjust after the first year.

If it feels like too much, that's usually a sign it's about right.

Step 3: pay the quarterly estimates from it

If you pay estimated taxes each quarter, pay them straight from this account. Whatever's left at the end of the year covers the final bill, and anything extra is a bonus.

Make it automatic. Many banks let you set a rule to move a percentage of every deposit. Set it once and forget it.

When the bill arrives, the money is already sitting there. Tax season turns from a scramble into a single transfer, which is exactly how boring it should be.
