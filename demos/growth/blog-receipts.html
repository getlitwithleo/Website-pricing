5 receipts small businesses forget to keep

Most owners keep the big receipts. It's the small ones that slip away, and they add up to real money at tax time.

Rachel Kim · Founder, ClearpathOct 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Ask any bookkeeper what goes missing at tax time and you'll hear the same answer: not the new van or the laptop, but the twenty-dollar purchases nobody thought twice about. One at a time they're nothing. Over a year, for a typical small business, they're often worth a few thousand dollars in deductions.

The five that go missing most

  1. Parking and tolls on work trips. Client visits, supply runs and site visits all count. Most parking apps can email you a receipt automatically.
  2. Software billed to a personal card. That design app, the scheduling tool, the extra cloud storage. If it's for the business, keep the invoice.
  3. Small tools and supplies bought in a hurry. The extension cord from the hardware store, the printer ink, the box of zip ties.
  4. The business share of your phone bill. If you use your phone for work, part of the bill is a business cost. Keep the monthly statements.
  5. Coffee and lunch meetings. Note who you met and why on the back of the receipt, or in your phone, the same day.

The ten-second habit

Snap a photo of every receipt the day you get it and drop it into one folder or one email address. That's it. You don't need to sort it, rename it or file it. Your bookkeeper (or future you) can match it to the bank statement later.

Ten seconds now saves an afternoon of digging through your car, your coat pockets and your inbox in March.
