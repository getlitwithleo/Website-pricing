A two-location bakery got its evenings back

Business
Bakery, 2 shops
Staff
14
Plan
Monthly + payroll
Client since
2023

The problem

The owner was doing the books after closing, three nights a week, across two shops, two bank accounts and a pile of supplier invoices. She knew the business was doing well. She didn't know which shop was paying for which.

What we did

We connected both bank accounts and both card machines to one set of books, with each shop tracked separately. The six-month backlog was sorted in the first three weeks. Supplier invoices now go to a single email address, and we match them to payments every week.

The result

About six hours a week back, a one-page report by the 10th of every month, and a clear answer to the question she'd been asking for two years: the second shop was more profitable, and the first needed a price rise on wholesale orders.

A landscaping crew found $3,200 in unpaid invoices

Business
Landscaping
Staff
6, seasonal
Plan
Monthly bookkeeping
Client since
2024

The problem

During the busy season, invoices went out by text, email and sometimes on the back of a receipt. Nobody was tracking which had been paid, and by October the owner had a feeling money was missing.

What we did

We matched every invoice from the season against every payment that came in. Eleven had never been paid. We sent polite reminders on the owner's behalf, and set up invoicing from one place, with automatic reminders after 14 days.

The result

$3,200 recovered in the first month, which paid for more than a year of bookkeeping. This season, the longest any invoice has gone unpaid is 19 days.

2 weeks

A yoga studio went from a shoebox to tax-ready

Business
Yoga studio
Staff
3 + 8 instructors
Plan
Year-end, then monthly
Client since
2025

The problem

The studio had a full year of receipts in a literal shoebox, class payments coming in through three different apps, and a tax deadline five weeks away.

What we did

We scanned and sorted every receipt, pulled the payment reports from all three apps, and matched everything to the bank statements. Instructor payments were separated out so the tax preparer could see who was staff and who was a contractor.

The result

Tax-ready books in two weeks, handed to the preparer with three weeks to spare. The studio moved to monthly bookkeeping so it never happens again, and the shoebox is now a plant pot.
