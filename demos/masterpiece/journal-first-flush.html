A week in the hills at first flush

Ten days, one valley, and the first tea of the year. Notes from a week in Darjeeling in March.

By Mei Lin · April 2026 · 6 minute read

The road up to Kurseong climbs two thousand meters in about three hours, most of it in fog. When it clears, every slope you can see is tea: tight, dark green rows wrapping the hills like contour lines on a map.

I arrived on the second day of first flush. The bushes had been dormant all winter, and now, for about ten days, the whole valley was picking. Women moved along the rows with baskets on their backs, taking only the top two leaves and a bud, faster than I could follow with my eyes.

Two leaves and a bud

By late morning the baskets came down to the factory, where the leaves were spread out on long troughs to wilt. The smell was the first surprise: not grassy, like green tea, but sweet and almost floral, like cut apples.

"The cold nights make the tea," the garden manager told me. "The leaves grow slowly, so they keep everything."

Tasting at the source

Every afternoon, the day's batches were brewed in a row of white cups and tasted, slurped and spat out, one after another. By the fourth day I could tell the morning's picking from the afternoon's. By the sixth, I'd chosen the lot we now sell.

It's bright, brisk and has that grape-like muscatel note Darjeeling is famous for. When you drink it, you're drinking one week in March, on one hillside, picked by hand.
