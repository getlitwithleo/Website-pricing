Small Hours

Five night paintings about city light, each made between 1 and 4 in the morning, when the streets belong to buses, shop signs and rain.

The story behind the work

After Tidewater I wanted to leave the water behind, but I couldn't leave the timing. So I swapped the tide table for an alarm clock, and for one winter I walked the city between 1 and 4 in the morning.

At that hour, light behaves differently. Shop signs stay on for nobody. The last bus is lit up like a living room. Sodium streetlights turn puddles gold. I'd sketch for an hour, sleep for three, and paint before the memory went.

The paintings are louder than Tidewater: more color, harder edges, faster marks. Night in the city isn't quiet. It just sounds different.
